## 1. Two corrections to the handoff brief

### 1.1 There is no Figure 5 memory-traffic simulation to check against

The brief states that "the device-calibrated simulation (memory traffic vs. k,
energy, latency) already lives in Figure 5" and asks for a consistency check
against "the 5x/10x/20x reductions that appear in the Figure 5 simulation at
k = 5, 10, 20".

**No such simulation exists in this repository.** There is no code, CSV or
document that computes traffic, bytes moved, energy (pJ/nJ/uJ) or latency, for
any k. What exists is:

- per-query **access counts** — `write_stats` in `online_accelerator_device.py`
  (:1207-1234), summarised by `scripts/analyze_write_stats.py`;
- **capacity** as a function of top_k — `docs/memory_footprint_reference.md`
  §6, and its §7 analytical access-count formulas;
- the only time-like quantity in the repo, `t_episode = n_readout_total x T_END`
  (`scripts/analyze_write_stats.py:105`), which is a *retention* requirement,
  not a latency model.

No "5x/10x/20x" claim appears anywhere either. The consistency check requested
in §3.2 of the brief therefore has no counterpart; §3.2 below reports the
analytical ratio on its own terms instead. The manuscript's figure numbering has
also moved — the current Fig. 1 is retrieval/generation quality
(`docs/figure_legend_draft.md`) and the current Fig. 2 is the analog distance
primitive (`sweep_exports/Claude outputs/Figure2_caption.docx`); the
problem-definition panel described in the brief is an unfilled slot.

Unrelated but worth flagging: `Cover_Letter_NCS.docx` claims "24.7-fold higher
energy efficiency than a GPU baseline" and "a few kilobytes per query" for a
ColBERTv2 / MS MARCO pipeline. Nothing in this repository backs those numbers.

### 1.2 The ratio is ≈ (1 + k), not ≈ k

The candidate's raw d-vector is required **only** by the diversity term (the
relevance term comes from the PQ/LUT path), and it must reach the comparison
stage in **both** implementations. It therefore belongs in both totals, and the
brief's own formula is right:

```
T_sw (N, k, d)  = N*d + N*|S|*d        (broadcast + re-read S per candidate)
T_arr(N, k, d)  = N*d + N              (broadcast in, one scalar out)
ratio           = T_sw / T_arr  ~  1 + k
```

Measured (cross scope, n = 1000, calibration pass included):

| k | T_sw (elements/query) | T_arr (elements/query) | ratio | 1 + k |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 1.268e7 | 2.010e6 | **6.31** | 6 |
| 10 | 2.257e7 | 2.007e6 | **11.24** | 11 |
| 20 | 4.225e7 | 2.002e6 | **21.11** | 21 |

Any accounting that yields exactly 5/10/20 has dropped the broadcast from the
numerator while keeping it in the denominator. `fig2_totals.csv` also carries
`ratio_S_array_only` (S-array traffic only, excluding the broadcast from both
sides); that column is ~4.1e3 / 7.9e3 / 1.5e4 and is **not** comparable to a
`k` or `1+k` expectation — it is the ratio of S-array element traffic alone.

## 3. Measured inputs (n = 1000, hotpot q1000 build)

`sweep_exports/write_stats_k/<scope>/k<K>/<run_id>/write_stats_summary.json`.
The k = 5 runs reproduce the published `sweep_exports/write_stats/` runs
**bit-identically** on every counter, in both scopes — the k-sweep changed
nothing but top_k.

| scope | k | pool | N | \|S\|final | anchors | writes/query | calib readouts | N / writes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cross | 5 | 2,585 | 2,582.1 | 5.00 | 3 | 11.86 | 32 | 217.7 |
| cross | 10 | 2,585 | 2,578.1 | 10.00 | 7 | 20.10 | 32 | 128.3 |
| cross | 20 | 2,585 | 2,571.1 | 20.00 | 14 | 38.41 | 32 | 66.9 |
| distractor | 5 | 9.9 | 6.94 | 4.98 | 3 | 5.50 | 9.9 | 1.3 |
| distractor | 10 | 9.9 | 3.12 | 9.93 | 7 | 9.93 | 9.9 | 0.3 |
| distractor | 20 | 9.9 | **0.00** | 9.93 | 9.93 | 9.93 | 9.9 | 0.0 |

Four things the brief's model does not anticipate:

1. **N is essentially independent of k** in the cross scope (2,582 / 2,578 /
   2,571). The candidate stream is set by `nprobe x chunks-per-centroid`, not by
   k, so the whole k-dependence of the diversity term sits in `|S|`.
2. **Writes are not `k`.** Measured `writes_total` is 11.86 / 20.10 / 38.41 rows
   per query, i.e. **1.9x to 2.4x k** (anchors + appends + replacements). Using
   `k*d` for the write count understates array writes by roughly a factor of two,
   and `reads_per_write = N/k` overstates the read/write ratio by the same
   factor (526/263/131 assumed vs 218/128/67 measured).
3. **`|S| = k` exactly** at the end of every query in the cross scope
   (`buffer_rows_final` min = max = k), and the buffer saturates almost
   immediately (2-6 appends against ~2,580 streamed candidates). The worst-case
   bound `|S| = k` in §3.1 of the brief is therefore tight here, not conservative.
   Caveat: the per-step |S| trace is not persisted, so this is inferred from the
   endpoints; a `--dump_embeddings` run would measure it directly.
4. **The standardization pass is a separate, k-independent term.**
   `_mmr_diversity_core` (`online_accelerator_device.py:775`) performs
   `min(pool, 32)` extra `pair_matrix` calls per query before the main loop —
   32 in the cross scope, ~10 in distractor. It is counted in all totals here.

Also: the tau_dup gate is **not** the pre-write filter that does the work. Of
all rejections, 0.51-0.56 % come from tau_dup and 98.5-99.1 % from the score
gate (cross scope). The brief's §3.1 note attributes pre-write rejection to tau_dup.

### 3.1 The distractor scope collapses for k >= 10

At k = 20 the candidate pool (~10 paragraphs) is entirely consumed by the
`gamma = 0.7` anchor fill, so **N = 0**: nothing is streamed and MMR never runs.
At k = 10, N = 3.1 and the buffer cannot reach k either. The evidence is
decisive — the L2 and MMR arms return **bit-identical** retrieval metrics at both
k = 10 and k = 20 (recall 0.944 / ROUGE-L 0.9353 / ILD 0.4692 / near-dup 0.6512
for both arms), which they do not at k = 5.

Those two runs are marked `mmr_active = False` in every CSV and are excluded from
`fig2_tiers.csv`. **Figure 2b can show the distractor scope at k = 5 only.**

## 4. Tier coordinates and the lifetime gap

`reads_per_write` is defined **per cell** — how many times a cell is read
between two writes to that cell — which is the endurance-relevant quantity:

```
static  (centroids): read once per query, written once per rebuild
                     reads_per_write = Q_rebuild ;  lifetime = Q_rebuild

dynamic (S):         every readout touches every row, so a cell is read
                     rd_total times per query; a given row is rewritten
                     writes_total/|S| times per query
                     reads_per_write = rd_total * |S| / writes_total
                     lifetime        = |S| / writes_total   [queries]
```

Measured dynamic tier: reads_per_write 1,102 / 1,299 / 1,356 and lifetime
**0.42 / 0.50 / 0.52 queries** at cross k = 5/10/20 (15.2 and 0.90 at
distractor k = 5). The selected set does not survive a single query — it is
rewritten about 2.4 times within one, which is a stronger statement than the
brief's `lifetime = 1/r` and follows from the measured write count rather than
from the round count.

`Q_rebuild` has no value anywhere in the repository. Four scenarios are carried
explicitly as **assumptions**, at a stated query rate and rebuild cadence:

| scenario | Q_rebuild | lifetime gap (decades) | four orders? |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 edge / personal, nightly rebuild, 1e2 queries/day | 1e2 | 2.04 – 2.38 | no |
| S2 team, nightly rebuild, 1e3 queries/day | 1e3 | 3.04 – 3.38 | no |
| S3 enterprise, nightly rebuild, 1e4 queries/day | 1e4 | **4.04 – 4.38** | yes |
| S4 enterprise, weekly rebuild at 1e4 queries/day | 7e4 | 4.89 – 5.22 | yes |

**The manuscript's "four orders of magnitude" claim holds only for
`Q_rebuild >= ~9e3`** — i.e. an enterprise-scale deployment, or any deployment
rebuilding weekly rather than nightly. Across the full plausible range the gap
is **2.0 to 5.2 decades**. Either narrow the deployment assumption and state it,
or write "up to four orders of magnitude" / "two to five orders of magnitude
depending on rebuild cadence". Stating "four orders" unconditionally is not
supported by this sweep.

| scope | k | L2 near-dup rate | MMR near-dup rate |

| cross | 5 | **0.971** | 0.784 |
| cross | 10 | **0.960** | 0.807 |
| cross | 20 | **0.937** | 0.783 |
| distractor | 5 | 0.833 | 0.791 |

97 % of the pairs in a plain-L2 top-5 window are near-duplicates, and the figure

- **No per-query distribution exists.** `_avg_metrics`

  `docs/HANDOFF_mmr_evaluation.md:102` says cosine > 0.85; every call site passes

## 8. Stored-value precision per tier — how many bits per element (`b_e`, `b_c`)

`scripts/make_fig2_precision.py` -> `fig2_precision.csv`, `fig2_precision_bits.csv`;
panel from `RAG_MFMIS/plot_fig2_precision.py`.

A per-cell Gaussian write-resolution sigma is applied to the **stored** side of
each tier and the end-to-end retrieval change is measured over the same 1,000
HotpotQA queries. sigma* is the first sigma at which a metric falls 1 % below
its sigma = 0 value (log-interpolated, mean of 5 seeds).

**Bit convention, used everywhere from now on: `bit = log2(FS / 6*sigma)`** —
adjacent levels must be 6 sigma apart to be distinguishable. The repository
previously carried two conventions; `read_disturb_plots.py:1507` uses
`log2(FS/|dw|)`, which is 2.58 bit more optimistic. `fig2_precision_bits.csv`
emits that as `bits_1sigma_ref` for reference only.

### 8.1 Static tier (centroids), full scale 1.3106 V, exact re-rank

| bound | metric | sigma* | bits (6 sigma) |
|---|---|---|---|
| answer quality | MMR recall@5 | 108 mV | **1.02** |
| | MMR nDCG@5 / MRR / MAP@5 | 106-109 mV | 1.00-1.04 |
| | L2 recall@5 | 105 mV | 1.06 |
| returned set identity | top-5 set Jaccard vs ideal | 16.5 mV | **3.73** |
| ranking self-consistency | centroid rank Kendall tau | 3.2 mV | **6.09** |

Measured device resolution for comparison: 5.6 mV (R1C1) = 5.3 bit,
18.4 mV (R0C0) = 3.6 bit. The device therefore clears the answer-quality bound
by 19x-33x in sigma, clears the set-identity bound at R1C1 and sits just at it
at R0C0, and does not clear the strict rank bound.

### 8.2 Dynamic tier (selected set S), full scale 3 V (the +-1.5 V window)

Corrected 2026-09-20 from 6 V. Full scale here must be the span a stored
element can occupy -- a 4T2C node holds +-1.5 V -- not the +-3 V SL drive
swing. **The bit column did not move.** `FS_DYNAMIC` is used twice in
`make_fig2_precision.py`: it converts a write resolution in mV into embedding
units at the noise injection, and it divides the bit conversion. It therefore
cancels, and the bit count is the full-scale-free quantity

    bit = log2(x_span / 6*noise_emb)

What the correction moves is the volt label on sigma, which halves. No
re-simulation was needed: the sigma grid was halved together with the full
scale, so every injected noise level, every rng draw and every metric value in
`fig2_precision.csv` is exactly as measured. The 6 V-labelled copy is kept at
`fig2_precision.fs6v.csv`.

Reporting rule: a bit count below 1 is written **`< 1`**, never as a number.
`log2(FS / 6*sigma)` goes negative once 6 sigma exceeds full scale, and that is
not a resolution -- it is the statement that the tier imposes no resolution
requirement at all.

| bound | metric | sigma* | bits (6 sigma) |
|---|---|---|---|
| answer quality | MMR recall@5 | 400 mV | **< 1** |
| | MMR nDCG@5 | 437 mV | < 1 |
| | MMR full-support@5 | 169 mV | 1.57 |
| returned set identity | top-5 set Jaccard vs ideal | 7.6 mV | **6.03** |
| ranking self-consistency | candidate rank Kendall tau | 3.5 mV | **7.15** |
| control | L2 recall@5, L2 nDCG@10 | no crossing to 1,000 mV | - |

The tau row is new (2026-09-20). It is the dynamic-tier counterpart of the
static tier's centroid rank tau and is read the same way: same `-1 %` criterion,
same column in `fig2_precision_bits.csv`, same 200-query subsample (every 5th
query). What is ranked differs, because the two tiers rank different things.
The static tau ranks the centroids by query distance; the dynamic tau ranks the
50 candidates in the MMR window by redundancy against the selected set,
`min d(c, S)` written as `max_j cos(c, S_j)`.

`S` is pinned to the **ideal** selection for both the clean and the perturbed
ordering. Letting each run score against its own `S` would confound a change in
the ordering with a change in what the ordering is measured against. The
candidate window itself is stored-noise-free -- it comes from the clean
query-side scores -- so the list being ranked is identical run to run.

Caveat on this one number. tau falls from 1.000 to 0.991 at the very first
non-zero sigma (1 mV) and then decays slowly, so most of the 1 % is spent
flipping near-ties immediately and the crossing lands on a steep, sparsely
sampled part of the curve (between the 2.5 and 5 mV grid points). 3.5 mV should
be read as "a few mV", not to two significant figures. A grid below 1 mV would
pin it; the same caveat applies to the static tau at 3.2 mV, which sits in the
same regime.

The result worth stating: at the strictest bound the two tiers are nearly
identical in absolute terms, 3.2 mV static vs 3.5 mV dynamic. The dynamic tier's
apparent 1.06-bit advantage there (7.15 vs 6.09) is the full-scale ratio
log2(3 / 1.3106) = 1.19 bit, not an algorithmic difference -- exactly the trap
8.3 warns about.

The L2 control is the sanity check that the perturbation is correctly scoped:
the L2 arm never touches the selected set, and its metrics are flat across the
whole sweep, as they must be.

### 8.3 What this says, and the caveat that must travel with it

The honest cross-tier statement is about **answer-quality tolerance**, not
about bits: the dynamic tier tolerates **3.7x more stored-value error**
(400 mV vs 108 mV) before answer quality moves. The mechanism is the reason —
a centroid error changes *which candidates exist* (a hard gate on the pool),
while a selected-set error only reshuffles the diversity ranking inside a pool
that is already correct.

**Bits are not directly comparable across the two tiers**, because the full
scale differs (1.3106 V vs 3 V) and that is a device/window choice, not an
algorithmic one. At the set-identity bound the static tier tolerates about
2.2x more absolute error (16.5 mV vs 7.6 mV) yet reports 2.3 bit fewer, purely
through the window. Any sentence of the form "the static tier needs N bits and
the dynamic tier needs M" must state both full scales, or the comparison is an
artefact.

For Figure 2's byte-denominated counts: the requirement is **below 2 bits per
element at the answer-quality bound and below 7 bits at the strictest bound**,
unchanged by the full-scale correction for the reason given in 8.2,
so `b_e = b_c = 1 byte` (int8) is a safe upper bound and the brief's grid entry
of "1 (int8), 2 (fp16)" should be resolved to **1**.

### 8.4 Two discrepancies to resolve before this goes in the manuscript

1. **The numbers in `read_disturb_plots.py` disagree on two of three bounds.**
   Its header comment (lines 57-80) cites Kendall tau ~23 mV, Jaccard ~43 mV,
   recall@5 ~105-120 mV, and the STRICT/LENIENT bounds drawn in that figure are
   built on 23 mV and ~110 mV. This sweep reproduces recall (108 mV) but gets
   3.2 mV for Kendall tau and 16.5 mV for Jaccard — 2.6x-7x stricter. The
   definitions are probably not the same (this sweep takes Kendall tau over the
   full 128-centroid distance vector, sampled every 5th query, and Jaccard over
   the MMR-selected top-5 chunk rows; the earlier numbers may use the probed
   centroid set). **The STRICT bound in the read-disturb figure depends on
   which is right**, so this needs settling rather than picking one.
2. **The PQ regime's crossings are not trustworthy for low-baseline metrics.**
   `fig2_precision_bits.csv` reports `static/pq` sigma* = 2.2 mV for
   MMR full-support@5, but that metric's PQ baseline is 0.100, so a 1 % relative
   drop is one query in a thousand. Use the `exact` regime for every bound and
   treat the PQ column as a robustness check only.

### 8.5 Provenance and limits

- Both tiers use the retrieval proxy in `sweep_exports/tol_run/tol_probe.py`
  (IVF probe, cosine re-rank in the pool, Carbonell MMR over the top-50), the
  same proxy the published tolerance numbers came from. Its device baseline
  reproduces `tol_run/out/tol_summary_fs1p31_vml1p4.json` exactly
  (MMR recall 0.6680, L2 recall 0.7275). It is **not** the pipeline's two-phase
  on-the-fly MMR, so these sigma* values bound the proxy, not the shipped
  re-ranker; the shipped one has the anchor fill and the tau_dup / score gates.
- Query embeddings come from `sweep_exports/query_emb_q1000_raw.npy`. That dump
  encodes `query_concat or query`, but the pipeline encodes
  `_prepare_cuad_search_text(...)`, which prepends the first quoted phrase —
  **126 of the 1,000 queries differ**. For a resolution sweep, where every
  point is compared against a sigma = 0 baseline built from the same
  embeddings, this shifts the query set slightly but not the conclusion.
  `scripts/dump_query_embeddings.py` should be fixed before the dump is reused
  for anything absolute.
- The generator for the earlier `fig_igzo_resolution.png` was never in the
  repository, and `res_curve.py` never completed (its log stops at
  sigma = 3.5 mV and no `res_curve.json` was written). Both tiers are now
  produced by one script on one basis; the earlier PNGs should be regarded as
  superseded.
